# UrbanStyle Hiring Guide — Role A+D: Hiring Manager & Team Lead View

**Author:** Fatima Rahimi · **Week 9 group work** · **For:** Liis Koppel and UrbanStyle's leadership team

*General employer criteria for hiring junior data analysts — not an assessment of any specific person.*

---

## Why this matters for UrbanStyle

UrbanStyle is hiring 7 analysts across Tallinn, Tartu, Pärnu, Finland and Germany. With a distributed team of ~45 people, every new analyst must communicate clearly and manage their own time — nobody will be looking over their shoulder.

## 1. What the hiring manager looks at first (30-second CV scan)

1. **Headline and summary** — does it say "data analyst" and name the core tools (SQL, Python, Power BI)?
2. **Numbers in the bullets** — "analysed 10,000+ sales, found a segment worth €1.15M" beats "worked with data".
3. **A GitHub link** — and does it work?

## 2. CV and LinkedIn criteria

| Strong | Weak |
|---|---|
| Action verb + tool + result ("Built an automated pipeline in Python that…") | Lists of duties ("Responsible for reports") |
| Business impact in € or % | No numbers at all |
| 1–2 pages, simple layout, readable by ATS software | Tables, images, fancy fonts that software can't read |
| Previous career framed as an advantage | Previous career hidden or apologised for |

**Pin-worthy LinkedIn projects:** projects that answer a business question with a clear result — e.g. customer segmentation ("18% of customers bring 43% of revenue") or an automated reporting pipeline. Not tutorial exercises.

## 3. Team lead view: how will they work with us?

- **Communication** — can they explain a result simply to Anna in marketing, not just to Toomas in IT?
- **Time management** — do they deliver on time? Ask: *"Tell me about a deadline you nearly missed. What did you do?"*
- **Collaboration** — in group projects, do they describe what *the team* achieved and their part in it?
- **Independence** — do they try to solve problems first, then ask for help with a clear question?
- **Growth potential** — are they curious and keen to learn? A junior with energy grows faster than an expert who has stopped learning.

## 4. Green flags ✅

1. **Straightforward, clear communicator** — answers questions directly and explains their work without jargon.
2. **Enthusiastic and eager to learn** — even with less experience, they're excited by a new challenge. For a fast-growing company like UrbanStyle, this matters more than a perfect CV.
3. **Shows results, not just tasks** — their CV, portfolio and stories all end with a number or a business outcome.

## 5. Red flags 🚩

1. **Poor time management** — lets tasks slip past deadlines, or can't give an example of managing their own priorities. Especially risky for remote hires in Finland and Germany.
2. **Highly skilled but not a team player** — strong technically but talks only about "I", dismisses others' input, or avoids shared work. One brilliant loner can slow a whole team.

## 6. Recommendation to Liis

Give **communication and reliability** the same weight as technical skill. Skills can be taught in weeks; attitude and teamwork are much harder to change.
